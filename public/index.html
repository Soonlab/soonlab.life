Our Goal

What we are trying to find out

Two patients receive the same cancer treatment. One tumor disappears; the other barely changes. Our goal is to find the measurement that tells them apart in advance — and to be equally clear about when no such measurement exists in the data at hand.

A signature that separates responders in one cohort and fails in the next is not a weak biomarker. It is usually a sign that the question was asked at the wrong moment, or of the wrong measurement.

We spent a decade building the material — patient-derived organoids and cell lines across colorectal, pancreatic, breast and brain tumors — and now ask what such models can and cannot predict. When a signature does not reproduce, that becomes the finding.

Soon-Chan Kim works where clinical oncology data meets computational biology. He spent a decade at the Cancer Research Institute and Korean Cell Line Bank, Seoul National University College of Medicine, establishing patient-derived cancer cell lines and organoids, and joined the University of Suwon in 2025. He now applies multi-omics and machine learning to treatment response with clinical groups at Seoul National University Hospital, and serves as Academic Director of the Korean Cell Line Research Foundation.

Join Us

Graduate & undergraduate positions

We work with real clinical data, so the interesting part of the job is rarely the model. It is deciding what a number means, and when the honest answer is that the data cannot tell.

You need neither a biology degree nor strong programming to start, only the willingness to grow into both. Projects span organoid and biopsy transcriptomics, deep learning on pathology slides, and bacterial genomics.

What helps

  • Python or R, enough to write your own analysis.
  • Statistics you can defend.
  • Curiosity about the biology.
  • Care with detail: most errors are bookkeeping errors.

To apply, email a short note on what you would like to work on, with a CV or transcript. Undergraduate interns are welcome.
